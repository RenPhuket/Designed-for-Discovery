DESIGNED BY THE LAND
THE STORY OF RENAISSANCE PHUKET
RESORT & SPA BEGINS WITH ITS SETTING
When planning for the resort began around 2006, the site at 
Mai Khao already presented a distinct landscape: a long 
beachfront setting, gentle changes in level and a lower area 
through the center of the property.
Rather than treating the landscape as something to be added 
after the architecture, it became part of the planning from the 
beginning. The buildings, villas, open spaces and landforms were 
considered together, allowing the character of the site to 
inﬂuence how the resort would take shape. 
Guestroom buildings were positioned along the sides of the 
property, leaving a generous landscaped space through the 
center. Pool villas were grouped together in a more private 
setting, while the landscape between them became an important 
part of the guest experience.

A STORY TOLD BY LOCALS
A WHITE TREE, A COASTAL LAND 
AND A WAY OF LIFE
In Mai Khao, local people have long spoken of a large white-barked 
tree that stood on a nearby hill and could be seen by passing vessls. 
The place was once called Mai Kaeo, the story goes, named after this 
distinctive tree, with the name later shiftting in local speech to Mai 
Khao. Its a story passed down in the community, reﬂecting how 
closely the landscape and local life have alwats been connected.
Why the dark wall?
The dark background was designed to contrast with the pale trunks of the 
Melaleuca, making the White-bark tree stand out within the landscape.
Melaleuca grows well in wet, low-lying environments and can be 
found in coastal areas and around peat-swamp margins in Southern 
Thailand. Its pale, layered bark gives the tree its distinctive 
appearance. 
Here, the tree is more than a familiar part of the landscape. 
For generations, its timber and bark have been valued for their 
practical qualities and have supported everyday life along the coast.

SPREAD 4
A SEQUENCE OF DISCOVERY
THE RESORT WAS NEVER INTENDED 
TO REVEAL ITSELF ALL AT ONCE.
From the beginning, the journey through Renaissance Phuket Resort & Spa 
was considered as carefully as the individual spaces themselves.
Arrival begins with architecture. From the lobby and The Lounge, the view 
starts to open. Larger trees and denser planting frame the sides, while the 
centre remains more open, drawing the eye deeper into the property.
Move forward and the landscape takes over. Paths curve rather than 
revealing every destination directly. Water appears between planting. Pale 
sand begins before the beach itself. Only gradually does Mai Khao Beach 
become part of the view.
The original landscape concept describes this as a “sense of discovery” 
expressed through visual sequence—drawing guests through the resort 
rather than concentrating the experience in one place. 
The sequence was designed to make each part of the resort lead to another:

SPREAD 6
FOLLOW THE WATER
WATER HAS ALWAYS BEEN PART OF 
THE LANDSCAPE
Water ﬁrst appears at the lobby entrance, where the fountain introduces it as 
part of the arrival experience. Its placement also reﬂects Feng Shui, in which 
ﬂowing water is associated with positive energy, prosperity and abundance, 
making it an auspicious element at an entrance.
From here, water becomes a recurring part of the landscape. Streams run along 
both sides of the guestroom buildings, following the natural fall of the land 
toward the centre of the property, where they meet the pond.
During the rainy season, this movement also serves a practical purpose: 
rainwater drains into the streams and follows the lower ground toward the 
pond, which occupies a naturally lower part of the site.
From the symbolic water at arrival to the streams and pond beyond, water 
connects the journey through the landscape.

Quan Spa sits close to the centre of Renaissance Phuket Resort & Spa, yet 
it was deliberately designed to feel secluded. Raised landforms and dense 
tropical planting surround the spa, creating privacy and separating it 
from the more open spaces of the resort.
The landscape draws inspiration from Phuket’s tropical interior, where 
greenery, changes in elevation and water create a more enclosed 
environment. Here, the land rises around the spa, allowing much of the 
building to remain hidden from view.
Water also plays a role in the spa’s identity. The name Quan comes from a 
Chinese expression meaning “the source of pure water.” This connection 
continues inside, where the eight treatment rooms are named using 
words for water from diﬀerent languages.
The result is a space where the landscape provides privacy, while water 
connects the architecture to the spa experience.

SPREAD 7
CAN YOU SEE THE TURTLES?
A ROOFLINE INSPIRED BY 
A NESTING SHORE
Mai Khao's association with nesting sea turtles became one of 
the references explored in the architecture of Renaissance 
Phuket Resort & Spa. Rather than reproducing the form of a 
turtle literally, the idea was translated more subtly through the 
softened roof forms and natural materiality of the villas. 
It is a detail that can be easy to miss at ground level.
From a higher viewpoint, however, the relationship becomes 
easier to see: individual roof forms sit among the landscape 
rather than forming a single continuous architectural block.
It is one of the quieter examples of how something found 
beyond the resort's boundary inﬂuenced what was designed 
within it.

SPREAD 8
ROOMS REIMAGINED
REDEFINING SOUTHERN SEASIDE FOLKWAYS
The renewed guestrooms and pool villas draw inspiration from 
Southern Thailand's coastal heritage, translating traditional 
craftsmanship, materials and everyday objects into contemporary 
interior design.
Completed in 2025, the renovation follows a concept described as 
“Redeﬁning the Southern Seaside Folkways.” It draws on the practical 
knowledge passed through generations, where architecture and 
everyday objects were shaped by local materials, climate and the needs 
of daily life.
Rather than recreating traditional interiors, the design reinterprets 
familiar elements through contemporary forms and functions. Privacy 
screens and breathable walls, once practical features of Southern 
vernacular architecture, provide inspiration for details within the 
renewed spaces.
In the guestrooms, the design also takes cues from traditional Thai houses. 
Wood ceiling details reference the living spaces beneath these structures, 
while a palette of sand and ocean tones reﬂects the contrast between Mai 
Khao's shoreline and the Andaman Sea.

SPREAD 9
Look around the lobby and discover two sculptural forms that blur the line 
between furniture and art. Created by artist and designer EggWong, the 
Hydra Collection was designed exclusively for Renaissance Phuket Resort & 
Spa in 2010, exploring the relationship between man and woman through 
contrasting yet complementary forms.
Look closer and you’ll ﬁnd inspiration from the coast. Their organic shapes 
reference coral, cuttlebone and sea cucumber, reinterpreted through 
abstract forms.
Crafted from anodized aluminium, stainless steel and marble, the pieces 
feature a neutral palette chosen to complement the natural surroundings of 
Mai Khao.
The Pool V illas draw inspiration from Southern folk art, reinterpreting 
traditional ﬁshing tools, ceramic dishes and wicker lanterns through 
contemporary decorative details. In the Deluxe Rooms, ﬁshermen’s rope knots 
inspire wall decorations, bringing a familiar element of coastal life into the 
interiors and connecting the design to Mai Khao’s seaside heritage.
Pearls introduce another layer of local identity. Long associated with the 
Andaman coast, their rounded forms and reﬂective qualities are referenced 
through decorative lighting, while wooden screens draw inspiration from 
traditional Southern Thai houses. Their angled panels reinterpret breathable 
walls and privacy screens, allowing light and air to pass through while 
maintaining privacy. Together, these elements bring coastal inﬂuences and 
practical architectural ideas into the renewed interiors.
